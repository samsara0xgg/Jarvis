Talk over him. Then let him act.

Say “Hey Jarvis” or tap the watch, in English or Chinese, and exactly one device answers. Cut in whenever you like, then let him act: mail, plans, reminders, buses and your Mac.

He yields the moment you speak. He gets quieter at once. After 0.8 s of your voice, what you said decides: a hum and he carries on, “stop” and he stops, anything else and he stops to answer.

One brain at home. Four bodies.

The brain is a Raspberry Pi at home that holds the memory, the event log, the model calls and the tools. Your iPhone, Apple Watch, iPad and Mac are his bodies, each with its own microphone, wake word and speaker.

How he’s built. One log, rebuilt into everything else.

Everything he knows flows one way: raw events in, one memory of you out. Every layer above the event log can be rebuilt from it.

No Jarvis cloud. Your record stays home.

Jarvis keeps a record of your days, so where that record lives matters more than any feature. There is no telemetry, no crash reporting and no update check.
