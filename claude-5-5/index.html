Nice to meet you.

I’m Allen Shi, a fourth-year student at UVic. Most AI agents only know what you tell them. Jarvis, my personal AI agent, records my days by himself, so he actually knows me. I build him alone, with Claude Code.

What you just saw. My Mac, then my home.

He lives beside my Mac’s notch, and everything he knows lives at my home.

Today

My day, as he saw it. Every block is something he noticed on his own: where I was, what I worked on, how I slept.

Why am I tired?

He answers from my week, not from a guess, and every answer points back to the moments it came from.

Agents

My Claude Code sessions, in one place. When one asks to run a command, the request drops down by the notch, and I allow or deny it there.

Memory

Who I am, in his words. One memory of me that he rewrites every night, and every line says where it came from.

Home

The real Jarvis is a Raspberry Pi at my home. It runs all day on less power than a light bulb. My days and my memories live there, not in someone’s cloud.

Why keep every day? It adds up.

It’s called lifelogging, and with Jarvis it takes no effort: I just wear the watch.

“AI gets better every few months, but it can only understand me as well as what I kept. A day I don’t record, I can never get back.”

Every record stays on the Pi at my home. When he thinks or speaks, only the piece he needs goes out: to Claude to think, and to speech services for his ears and voice.

Next, I want him to see the world around me too. Every frame will wait in a buffer while a small model removes anyone who didn’t agree to be recorded, before anything is saved. Not blurred: removed.
