One device answers.

The Mac listens for “Hey Jarvis” on its own; the iPhone and iPad find it in what they are already transcribing; the watch is a tap. When several are near you, the brain has already picked one before you speak: the one you are talking to, else the one you used last.

Hearing stays on the device.

Each device owns its microphone, wake word, echo cancellation, end-of-speech and playback; the brain never touches a mic or speaker. With a reSpeaker array his voice is removed in hardware; with any other mic, in software (WebRTC AEC3). Each line carries a voiceprint, so a video playing in the room does not start a conversation.

Words as you say them.

Your voice streams through the brain to Qwen; the key lives only on the brain. The captions are what Qwen has heard so far, so what you see is what the turn will use. If the brain or Qwen is unreachable, the Mac falls back to SenseVoice locally.

He speaks while he thinks.

The answer streams as it is written, and his voice (MiniMax, streamed over WebSocket) starts before the last word exists. A tool that takes a moment gets one short line first, like “Checking the weather”.

Talking over him.

He drops to 20% of his volume the moment you speak. After 0.8 s of your voice he holds his place and your words decide: a hum and he carries on, “stop” and he stops, anything else and he answers that. Why 0.8 s: at 0.4 s, a 0.61 s “mm” cut him off.

Quiet ends it.

Once open, the conversation needs no wake word. 10 seconds of quiet closes it: two 20-minute recordings of a lived-in room held 40 and 183 lines nobody meant for it. Say “hold on” and it waits a minute.
