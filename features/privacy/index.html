Privacy

Jarvis keeps a record of your days, so where that record lives matters more than any feature. The design starts from three rules.

Your data lives on a machine you own

Section titled “Your data lives on a machine you own”

The brain is a Raspberry Pi on your own network. The event log, the memory and the raw recordings are on it, and every other device is a body that talks to it. There is no Jarvis cloud. There is also no telemetry, no crash reporting, no usage statistics and no update check. Jarvis never contacts a server that belongs to it.

Nothing is read until you grant it. Each data source needs its own permission on the device it comes from: location on the phone, Health on the phone, screen recording on the Mac, and so on. If a source goes quiet when it shouldn’t, the nightly check tells you, instead of leaving a silent gap in your record.

Your devices reach it only over your private network

Section titled “Your devices reach it only over your private network”

The Mac, iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch reach the brain over Tailscale. The brain listens on localhost and on the private addresses you list, and nowhere else. It never listens on 0.0.0.0, and it refuses a public address. The setting is runtime.listen_addresses, and it is empty by default, so a fresh brain answers only on its own machine.

The watch has no token and no line of its own. It talks to the phone, and the phone relays to the brain.

What leaves goes only to services you choose

Section titled “What leaves goes only to services you choose”

Some things have to leave, because the models run elsewhere:

  • Model providers, with your own keys. Claude for thinking, Qwen for hearing your words, MiniMax for its voice. A key is sent only to its own provider. The keys live on the brain, not on the devices.
  • What its tools call. Web search, weather and BC Transit timetables, when you ask for something that needs them.
  • What you read into a conversation. A mail, a web page or a screenshot you bring up is sent with the next request to the model provider.

The wake word runs on the device, so nothing is sent until you start a turn. Your words are then heard by Qwen, sent through the brain; without the brain or its key, the on-device recognizer hears them instead and the audio stays on the device.

The specification lists every outgoing request, who it goes to, what is in it and when it is sent, and a new outgoing request has to change that chapter in the same commit.

The diagnostics that would keep speech, a recording of what it said and a log of what the models were sent, are off by default and have to be switched on by hand.
